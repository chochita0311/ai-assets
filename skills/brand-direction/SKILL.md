# Brand Direction

Make an art direction the reviewer can see, select and use on another screen. Work in review specimens until the user authorizes adoption. When asked for research, a checkpoint or a critique, deliver that scope directly. A general ai-assets export includes the study’s reusable public design references among its candidates. For that part of the export or a request for references alone, follow [the source library’s contribution instructions](references/design-sources.md#keep-useful-discoveries).

## Start or resume

- Identify the product, the people using it and the ordinary moment worth designing for. Inspect the current screen with representative content. Preserve the facts, reading order, accessibility and task constraints that make the example believable. Use locally held or synthetic material as appropriate.
- For a new direction, find one meaningful tension in the product. A crowded departures board, for example, must feel calm while making a changed platform unmistakable. Write what the eye should meet first and what should recede. Adjectives such as “premium” still leave both decisions open.
- For a return visit, reopen the exact specimen and the reviewer's decisions, including appearance, reading state and content. A reviewer pointing at an object may be endorsing its material, its placement or the atmosphere around it while leaving the literal mark open. Keep that distinction in the next brief. Current user decisions govern the brief. Existing product requirements bound what can change.

## Turn references into compositions

- When a relevant work is already known or a discovery is worth keeping, use the optional [design source library](references/design-sources.md). Search by the problem or material and follow a promising lead beyond the index whenever the brief calls for it. Keep the project’s use and the reusable source observation with their respective owners.
- Follow a gallery entry to the work or the maker's account. Identify a visible relationship that produces the desired impression and where the analogy breaks. An interface can supply a density or hierarchy precedent. A book series, sign system or film still can supply a different way of composing the product's own material. If a source is inaccessible, distinguish the description you could inspect from the visual decisions you could see.
- When exploring a new identity or a request for high-end art direction, consider current artists, studios and visual culture beyond the named maker alongside a precedent for the person's task. Trace a contemporary reference to a specific work and say what its date belongs to. Try the expressive relationship where it can do real work, then decide what the reader needs in the next moment. See [contemporary work and the reader's attention](references/visual-character.md#contemporary-work-and-the-readers-attention).
- Use the reviewer's reference collection as a taste sample. Look at the actual works side by side. Find a formal choice that recurs and a work that handles it differently. Pick the relationship that best suits the product's ordinary task. Bring a particular crop, scale contrast, alignment or use of empty space into a real composition and show where the influence ends. A prestigious gallery or a repeated style label gives little evidence on its own.
- Give the product a recognizable visual character. A photograph can earn its place through its silhouette, light and texture. An accent can connect that image to an ordinary working state. Data-bearing graphics also need an intelligible relationship to the data. Decide where expression should lead and where it can fall quiet. For included artwork, icons or textures, use original work or record the source, applicable distribution rights and obligations with the handoff.

### Choose a comparison for the open question

- For a collection of unrelated briefs, put the first rough compositions together while their grounds and layouts are still open. Identify which surface carries each product’s identity and what fills the viewport after the action. When a field or arrangement keeps returning, compare it with a credible alternative from that brief. A favored earlier collection can supply both an expressive and a quiet example. See [comparing the fields across briefs](references/visual-character.md#when-different-briefs-look-the-same-color). Reopen the exact earlier frames when a reviewer prefers their mood.
- When unrelated products inherit similar inks or dividing lines, keep the subject and the facts fixed. Compare where the color repeats and what each visible edge actually connects. Ask separately which treatment carries the atmosphere and which makes a particular decision easier to read. See [assigning ink and keeping a meaningful line](references/visual-character.md#assign-the-ink-and-question-the-line).
- If an accent feels foreign only after selection, follow the same item from its untouched state through selection and, where applicable, confirmation. Compare changing just the ink with changing how much of the object carries it. Verify what “chosen” means beside the other statuses. See [the color that appears on selection](references/visual-character.md#the-color-that-appears-on-selection).
- When a color feels foreign to a material, or an expressive stroke feels pasted on, use [color at the point of contact](references/visual-character.md#color-at-the-point-of-contact). Compare the same object with a different neighbor, find what the hand or eye can follow, and keep the reviewer's material-specific objection in scope.
- When a reviewer likes the colors but misses the subject, a value or a change, reopen that exact frame. Identify the fact they were trying to find and the object that drew their eye. Try changing the neighboring field, the stroke of the relevant letters or the placement of the emphasis while keeping the liked material visible. See [making one fact stand out](references/visual-character.md#make-one-fact-stand-out).
- When the palette works but the product feels anonymous, apply [composing around the work](references/visual-character.md#compose-around-the-work). Give a product fact a specific typographic and spatial role, put the action where that relationship is used, and show how it changes on a second real surface. Compare the actual target-script glyphs and composition with the closest existing study.
- When the work feels distinctive mainly because of its subject, test it beside a product that uses the same material for a different purpose. Use a new item on a second surface and inspect what the recognizable treatment leads the person to believe. See [when two identities share the material](references/visual-character.md#when-two-identities-share-the-material).

### Refine the composition

- Set the candidate beside its closest reference. If the subject, lettering and composition together still point to the reference's brand, rework the most recognizable borrowing using material from this product.
- When the choice is open, make genuinely different compositions from the same content. Change what organizes the page and where the eye enters. A different swatch or font on the same arrangement seldom resolves that decision. Once a direction is chosen, make a focused revision against its previous specimen.
- Set the actual language, writing system and content lengths at viewing size. Work the title breaks, align type and numerals by eye, decide where a line or space earns a division, and inspect the dominant shape at a glance. Check the ordinary row, paragraph and small state as closely as the lead image. [Check a short instruction](references/visual-character.md#when-a-short-instruction-wraps) at the width where it wraps and read it aloud. Keep a number, its object and its action intelligible when the user enlarges the text. Let product content occupy the focal point where the task calls for it.
- Before adopting a typeface, read [font use](references/font-use.md) to match the actual files and license to the specimen and intended release. Verify the rendered family for the product's scripts, numerals and code, including a fallback where coverage is incomplete.

## Show the work

For a saved study, use `<target-project>/.artifacts/brand-direction/` by default. Resolve the consuming project first and follow [the local workspace convention](references/artifact-workspace.md) for the entry page, revisions, relative links and Git exclusion. A spoken critique can stay in the conversation.

Deliver a rendered comparison in the project's review location. Choose a local HTML sheet, images or another inspectable format suited to the decision. Keep the baseline recoverable and the proposals distinguishable. Show the same meaningful content, screen state and viewport for each candidate. If light and dark are appearances of one identity, hold the direction constant across both. Start with stills. Add motion when its contribution is under review.

Open the work at its intended size before asking for a decision. Compare a representative crop with the admired work at a similar scale. Check where your eye lands, how the type sits against an edge and how the quieter parts hold together. Revisit the longest title and a quieter passage. Repair a competing accent, an awkward line break or a heavy border where it undermines the concept. Drop a weak alternative. If rendering is unavailable, label the proposed composition and its unverified appearance.

Briefly tell the reviewer what to look at, which direction you recommend and which quality it gains or gives up against the strongest earlier frame. Attach the judgment to a visible example. A gain in readability can be adopted separately if the old version still carries the intended atmosphere better. When the masthead scrolls away, look for a quiet connection to the same identity in the working screen. Keep measured rendering, the reviewer's taste and observed user performance separate.

For a requested independent critique or a study of repeatable quality across briefs, use [reviewing a set of studies](references/reviewing-studies.md). Keep first judgments separate from informed comparisons. When a disagreement would change the design, show competing treatments of the same working state and retain the argument for each.

## Use the review

Treat “something feels off” as sufficient feedback to continue. Keep the reaction in the reviewer's words and the cause provisional. Inspect the exact state beside the last version they preferred and a relevant reference. Form plausible, visibly different explanations. A section might feel fragmented because every paragraph is framed. A question might be hard to find because it has the same weight as the reply. Test the most informative difference on the same passage, holding the liked parts steady. Where both explanations remain plausible, show the alternatives in place. State your preference and the trade-off.

A reaction such as “the second feels closer”, “neither”, or “I can't tell” is a complete response. When neither helps, revisit the organizing idea or the reference selection. When the difference is imperceptible, keep the checkpoint and show a more discriminating comparison if exploration continues. Leave adoption open. Take responsibility for the visual diagnosis and keep a one-off reaction local to the specimen until the reviewer confirms a broader preference.

Record the exact specimen, the scope of a decision and the question for the next pass in the project's review record. Keep the viewable specimen with that record. Use the decisions below when the reviewer actually expresses one. An uncertain reaction leaves the direction open.

| Reviewer decision | Next move |
| --- | --- |
| Go on a named choice | Carry that choice forward within its scope. If extension is wanted, apply it to a representative, previously unseen surface or content extreme and show how it holds up. |
| Revise or partial Go | Keep the accepted parts visible. Translate the objection into an observable change and compare the revision with the retained version. If “too busy” could mean competing focal points or tight spacing, separate those readings visually when the difference matters. |
| No-Go | Preserve the last accepted checkpoint or the original baseline. If exploration continues, retire the rejected premise and try a materially different organizing idea. A request to stop ends the review. |

If transferring a choice weakens reading or hides a product distinction, show the failing screen and narrow or reopen the relevant choice with the reviewer. A reviewer can close a study on its atmosphere alone. Record recognizability across other applications when the reviewer has seen them.

## Carry a selected direction into a product

Give the design owner the exact specimen and state, the endorsed relationship, the traits that must repeat or can vary, and any artwork, motion or sample controls still open. Start each receiving project from its own content and brief. Find where the relationship would do useful work on a real screen and check the product’s existing design and interaction rules there.

For a visual refactor, use the selected specimen as the visual target for the working product. When the brief retains the shell, keep the placement and purpose of navigation regions such as the header and sidebar. Restyle those surfaces within scope and recompose each page through typography, spacing, icons, backgrounds and color. Preserve the information shown, available actions, navigation destinations, submitted data and resulting states. If a detail in the specimen conflicts with a retained behavior, find another way to express the same visual quality.

- **Tokens.** Map recurring properties such as type, spacing or color to their roles in the product. Check what a shared value already communicates and where else it is used. An accent that locates an item in the specimen may already signal a warning in the product.
- **Components and layout.** Show how the relationship fits the actual content, controls and available space. A full-height tool might carry the lighting and accent of a spacious specimen into its tab strip while preserving room for the work. Compare a proposed arrangement with the current one at the size or state where the choice matters.
- **Behavior.** Trace what the person can find, activate and return to. If a visual move would change grouping or order, keyboard focus, the action’s result or what persists, say what should stay and what would change. Show a behavior-preserving option when it helps decide the trade-off. Carry forward changes already authorized and leave consequential open decisions with the product owner.

Name unresolved exceptions and the affected states that still need a rendered or working check. When available and requested, use `$init-design` to establish or revise durable tokens, component and layout rules, or `$design-plan` to resolve shared impact, layout or interaction choices, rollout and verification. Product implementation follows the receiving project’s scope.

Compare the old and new working screens of a visual refactor on the same content and task. Set them beside the selected specimen and point to what gives the new screen the endorsed impression during use. Revise the visual treatment if that quality has faded. When implementing, verify preserved behavior on the actual product by repeating the task from the same starting state and comparing its outcome.
