# Contextual Design Patterns

Use these lenses to explain why a particular screen feels interchangeable, cluttered, or unfinished. A familiar motif alone is not a defect and cannot establish who or what created a design.

Judge the surface by its job: helping someone act, understand, compare, decide, or experience the work. The same treatment can support one job and obstruct another. Use a pattern only when target evidence supports its consequence; these are proposal directions, not automatic removal rules or a scoring system.

## Interchangeable composition

- **Symptom:** A large generic introduction, equal feature tiles, a proof strip, and another call to action structure the screen regardless of its content.
- **Why here:** The sequence postpones the actual task, evidence, or artifact that makes this product worth visiting.
- **Retain when:** A familiar sequence answers the audience's real questions in a useful order; conventional navigation and controls can reduce effort.
- **Directions:** Lead with an existing product artifact and supporting explanation; organize existing content around a task; or make a real comparison the compositional center. Choose by the visitor's need, not novelty alone.

## Hierarchy confused with repeated labels

- **Symptom:** Eyebrows, pills, subtitles, and headings repeat language while competing for attention, or real metadata is styled as decoration.
- **Why here:** Visual emphasis does not distinguish the main subject from its scope, status, provenance, or next action.
- **Retain when:** A label carries a separate role, such as category, date, source, status, or navigation context. Repeated words can still orient someone; textual overlap does not prove semantic duplication.
- **Directions:** Remove an ornamental echo; attach meaningful metadata to the subject it qualifies; or build a stronger title and quieter supporting layer. Preserve required labels and distinctions when changing their presentation.

## A wrapper around every fragment

- **Symptom:** Every sentence, statistic, and control sits in a rounded panel, sometimes inside another panel with the same visual weight.
- **Why here:** Container edges compete with relationships, waste space, or imply independent objects where the content belongs together.
- **Retain when:** Cards represent independently actionable items, movable objects, or real groups; nesting can express an actual containment or permission boundary.
- **Directions:** Use open groups with spacing and shared alignment; use rows or a comparison table for comparable records; or reserve a stronger container for the one group that needs isolation. Keep grouping and hit targets legible.

## Repetition without hierarchy

- **Symptom:** Every section uses the same grid, height, headline scale, image proportion, and amount of whitespace.
- **Why here:** Important differences disappear, and readers cannot tell a key decision or supporting detail from the rest.
- **Retain when:** Repetition makes records scannable or comparable, supports a predictable workflow, or intentionally lets a collection take precedence.
- **Directions:** Give one existing artifact a larger field; alternate compact evidence with more spacious explanation; or use a consistent dense list with a distinct summary. Vary emphasis where the meaning changes, not at arbitrary intervals.

## Generic copy and borrowed credibility

- **Symptom:** Broad promises, stock praise, unsupported counters, or polished-looking testimonials fill space without explaining this product.
- **Why here:** The screen withholds concrete value or presents evidence that the product cannot substantiate.
- **Retain when:** Familiar wording is the clearest accurate label, a claim is supported, or sample content is explicitly identified in an appropriate demo context.
- **Directions:** Use supplied product language and concrete outcomes; foreground existing evidence; or remove unsupported ornament and state the known limitation. Do not invent a more specific promise, customer, metric, or capability to improve the design.

## Controls and charts used as props

- **Symptom:** Filters, tabs, progress rings, sparklines, or buttons look interactive or authoritative but have no corresponding behavior or meaningful data.
- **Why here:** The visual suggests an action, precision, or system state the product does not provide.
- **Retain when:** The element performs the implied action, encodes real information, or is unmistakably a labeled illustration rather than the interface itself.
- **Directions:** Present the existing information without false affordances; distinguish an illustrative product preview from live controls; or make a supported action clearer. Adding a new feature or fabricating data is not a visual repair.

## Color and surface effects without a role

- **Symptom:** Accents, gradients, glows, blur, texture, and shadows are spread evenly or borrowed from a fashionable style.
- **Why here:** They obscure priority, weaken contrast, suggest nonexistent layers, or conflict with the use environment and chosen identity.
- **Retain when:** The treatment supports recognition, an intentional material or expressive world, a meaningful state, or a useful depth relationship.
- **Directions:** Concentrate an existing accent on a decision; simplify layering around dense content; or amplify one identity-bearing surface while quieting its neighbors. Preserve state distinctions, readable contrast, and non-color cues.

## Typography as a costume

- **Symptom:** Oversized headings, extreme tracking, uniform weights, or a supposedly technical typeface substitute for a clear information structure.
- **Why here:** Reading order, data comparison, real text lengths, or the product's voice suffer even if the type looks striking in isolation.
- **Retain when:** Expressive lettering is part of the chosen identity, a familiar face serves sustained reading, or numeral and monospace forms support the actual content.
- **Directions:** Rebuild hierarchy using the existing family and spacing; give one title a stronger voice; or separate prose from dense data through measure and alignment. A new font is an optional design choice, not a prerequisite for character.

## Motion with no informational purpose

- **Symptom:** Every section enters the same way, several elements move at once, or decorative movement delays usable content.
- **Why here:** Attention goes to choreography instead of the user's task, and repeated effects erase the distinction between events.
- **Retain when:** Motion explains continuity, confirms an action, reveals a relationship, or is an intentional part of the experience people came to see.
- **Directions:** Keep functional transitions and reduce competing motion; concentrate expression in one meaningful moment; or use a still composition with clear states. Match the surface's job and preserve reduced-motion and interruption behavior.

## Minimalism that removes the product

- **Symptom:** Large empty regions, faint text, sparse generic headings, and absent context make a screen look clean but hard to understand or use.
- **Why here:** Necessary relationships, evidence, navigation, or working density have been removed along with decoration.
- **Retain when:** Space creates useful focus, a calm reading pace, or room for an artifact; sparse content may reflect the actual state rather than an unfinished design.
- **Directions:** Restore existing supporting context near the decision; increase useful density and typographic contrast; or let a distinctive existing artifact occupy the space. Restraint need not mean grayscale, uniform type, or empty content.

## Identity confused with every incumbent detail

- **Symptom:** Options preserve every wrapper and spacing choice, or swing to an unrelated aesthetic simply to look different.
- **Why here:** The first approach can protect incidental weaknesses; the second can discard the product's recognizable voice and the user's deliberate choices.
- **Retain when:** A motif, palette, type treatment, composition, or interaction is intentionally chosen or carries established meaning. Uncertain intent is a limit on the diagnosis, not proof that a detail is disposable.
- **Directions:** Preserve the defining relationships while changing the selected composition; amplify a real signature and simplify nearby clutter; or propose a clearly bounded alternative with its identity tradeoff stated. Bold change can rearrange existing content without new features, assets, frameworks, or a prescribed style.

## Sources and limits

Informed by the [Impeccable source](https://github.com/pbakaus/impeccable/tree/c8891a9ae865d6ee311836ae5199bfce0fe115b1/skill), particularly its entry contract and critique, craft-floor, quieter, and bolder references at the observed commit.
