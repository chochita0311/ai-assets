# Evidence and validation

Use this reference when source conflicts, empirical claims, or transferred methods can change the answer. Keep a compact working record appropriate to the task in the session or task-owned scratch; use an existing durable research owner only when persistence is already called for.

## Connect claims to materials

For each material claim, preserve its subject, relation, scope, conditions, and status: observed fact, hypothesis, unverified inference, counterevidence, or unresolved question. Connect it to supporting and opposing evidence and the reason for accepting, rejecting, or deferring it. Keep a rejected direction's reason and reopening condition so unchanged evidence does not restart it indefinitely.

Record evidence at the level needed to find and assess it: source and precise locator, origin or genealogy, observation or publication time, version **and** content fingerprint where obtainable, actual access level, relevant conditions, reliability concerns, and a reproduction procedure or its limit. A title or abstract is not full-text access; sampled frames are not an audio transcript. Missing fields remain unknown rather than becoming invented provenance.

Follow citations to primary material when it matters to the claim. Trace shared datasets, press releases, analyses, and citation chains before treating multiple documents as corroboration. Inspect contradictory or negative evidence with the same care as support. Resolve conflicts by relevance to the actual population, time, measurement, and question, source reliability, and reproducibility; explain unresolved conflicts instead of averaging incompatible claims or automatically favoring the newest document.

## Make methods earn their conclusions

For a chosen direction, connect the additional work a strong practitioner would do, the rawest usable material, the method and instrument, and the external observation that could support or contradict the result. Use the real technique when feasible. A field name, vocabulary change, or simulated expert opinion is not method transfer.

Before applying a borrowed method, check its assumptions, required data, identifiability or measurement conditions, and limits in the receiving domain. Select complexity for the question and data. When applicable, compare against a simple baseline or null explanation; do not assume a sophisticated model or a surprising story is better.

Specify the comparison, metric or qualitative decision rule, and acceptance or falsification conditions before inspecting the deciding outcome where feasible. Keep exploratory analysis labeled exploratory. If new evidence requires changing the rule, record why and the need for new validation; do not move the criterion merely to preserve a favored hypothesis.

Use the checks that bear on the claim, such as raw-data reproduction, counterexamples or negative controls, sensitivity to plausible assumptions, repeat measurements, or independent instruments. Verify the check itself: inputs, units, expected result, fixture, environment, and whether the observation distinguishes the explanations. Keep reproducible commands, settings, input versions and fingerprints, and output locators when computation is involved. A successful command establishes only what it actually measured.

## Keep kinds of independence separate

| Dimension | What to record | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reasoning context | Authoring history and conclusions available to each investigator; when findings were shared | A fresh context may reproduce the same mistake or use the same sources. |
| Explanatory or method diversity | Mechanisms considered, methods actually used, and applicability assumptions | Different role labels or models do not demonstrate distinct methods. |
| Source genealogy | Independent observations and any common upstream data or interpretation | Repeated citations to one origin are not separate corroboration. |
| Validation data | Which observations were used for exploration, selection, tuning, and final evaluation; access timing and restrictions | Recalculation of the same data is not independent outcome validation. |

For an empirical generalization, separate exploratory and selection material from untouched validation observations before using the latter to evaluate it. Specify the split or access rule and who may see what. Reusing a holdout to choose a method or revise a claim makes it part of development for that claim; record the contamination and obtain a new independent check when the acceptance condition requires one. Avoid temporal or information leakage between those roles.

If untouched data or an external instrument is unavailable, use the checks that remain useful and state their narrower evidentiary reach. Independent reasoning, same-data reproduction, sensitivity analysis, and untouched future observations answer different questions. None silently substitutes for another. A proposed experiment is a next step, not an observed result.

## Preserve an interpretable result

Report the comparison outcome, important failures and null results, and which explanations remain viable. Keep causal, predictive, and descriptive claims at the strength supported by the design. Distinguish a promising hypothesis from an established finding, and explain what differs from the existing baseline before calling an insight new.

When inputs, methods, criteria, or environment change, identify which earlier evidence still applies and which claims need rechecking. Retain opposing evidence and the reason for each material judgment in the continuation record. Reassess a planning allowance before treating it as a barrier to useful validation. If a limit blocks one check, continue other useful permitted validation or analysis. End with a bounded conclusion and a concrete next discriminating observation when no further useful validation or analysis is available within authority and actual limits; do not upgrade missing evidence into success.
