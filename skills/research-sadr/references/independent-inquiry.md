# Independent inquiry

Use this protocol for authorized fresh exploration, challenge, or adjudication. These are research responsibilities, not prescribed worker names or a fixed team. Check the actual role, model or capability binding, reasoning and modality needs, tools, permissions, and observed suitability before delegating. Optimize cost only after the competence requirement is met. A configured role is not proof that it ran or that its instructions impose a separate operating-system sandbox.

## Prepare discovery without supplying the answer

Give the worker a bounded task packet with:

- The research question, decision to inform, full constraints, relevant acceptance criteria, and permitted scope. Include material facts even when they weaken the author's view.
- Raw artifacts and source access, their identifiers, versions and content fingerprints where available, fixed comparison states where relevant, and environment or observation conditions. Identify inaccessible evidence and summaries as such. Do not substitute a selective author summary for available primary material.
- Allowed actions and tools, prohibited decisions, output or editing ownership where applicable, actual hard limits and their sources, a finite planning allowance and reassessment point, and the assignment's stopping or return conditions.
- The requested evidence return: investigated scope, methods and assumptions, findings or null findings, alternatives and counterevidence, source locators and access limits, observed resource use and remaining capacity, and the next discriminating check if needed.

An assigned worker returns at its stated boundary with the evidence and unresolved work. That boundary does not establish overall research sufficiency or require the primary to end the task. The primary reassesses the remaining work under the research boundary's resource rules; a critic may challenge whether the planned effort is adequate.

Do not give the author's rationale, preferred conclusion, expected finding list, prior answer, or authoring discussion to the initial independent discovery pass. If the user's question itself proposes an explanation, retain it as an attributed hypothesis that may be challenged. Do not hide an operative requirement to make the task appear neutral.

Use a new context without authoring history for a fresh claim. Assign independent questions or useful methodological contrasts, not tiny fragments that all assume the same explanation. Allow enough bounded source work and analysis to discover a different framing or approach. The primary agent can investigate another direction while preserving the independent workers' answer isolation. Where full conversational history is necessary for correctness, keep the work with the primary or label the worker as context-sharing; do not call it fresh.

Record what actually ran: worker identity, exposed model or capability setting, applicable instruction revision **and** content fingerprint, packet and artifact identities and fingerprints, inherited history, allowed and unavailable evidence or tools, and returned artifact. Retain observation limits when the runtime does not expose a field. Different models may add diversity, but are neither required nor a guarantee of independence; role labels and separate messages do not prove it either.

## Separate discovery from disputed-claim review

Let an independent explorer or critic finish and preserve their initial findings before revealing the author's synthesis or other workers' findings. This keeps the first discovery result distinguishable from later reactions. A critic looks for missing explanations, omitted evidence, invalid methods, and counterexamples; a critic is not required to find a defect.

After discovery, share the relevant claims, evidence, and the author's response for explicit adjudication. At this stage, concealing either side's material evidence harms the judgment. Track which conclusions arose independently and which emerged after exchange; a worker who has now read the author's position is no longer answer-isolated for that issue.

For each material dispute, examine:

- The competing claims and their exact predicates, population, time, conditions, and consequences.
- Direct supporting and opposing evidence, its access and provenance limits, and whether the cited observation reaches the disputed claim.
- The applicability of the method and the correctness of the proposed oracle, expected value, fixture, or environment. A failing check can reveal a faulty check.
- Whether a new source, reproduction, counterexample, simple baseline, or prospective observation can discriminate between the claims.

Use a separate capable adjudicator when a high-impact conflict, conflicting evidence, or the author's investment in a prior decision makes another judgment valuable. Provide both sides only after any requested independent discovery. A model adjudicator weighs evidence; it does not replace an objective test, and it can also be wrong. If that capability is unavailable, preserve the independence gap and bound the affected acceptance claim.

Keep claim validity and response action separate. Mark the claim supported, refuted, or unresolved; then record whether the synthesis or method was revised, accepted as an intentional change, retained with a reason, or deferred. Give reasons and remaining uncertainty when rejecting or deferring a material objection. Correct an invalid fixture before treating its failure as evidence against the hypothesis. Never decide by a majority vote, finding count, or pressure to accept criticism.

## Route a dispute into useful work

Return an adjudicated issue to the responsible author or investigator with its evidence, unresolved condition, and the decision a follow-up could change. Let the investigator revise the explanation or investigate further rather than merely editing wording. Depending on the issue:

| Evidence state | Useful return |
| --- | --- |
| A premise or task framing is contradicted | Revisit the question and affected assumptions; preserve the user's original scope. |
| Live explanations predict different observations | Obtain that observation or run the bounded discriminating experiment. |
| A field's method does not fit the data or assumptions | Choose an applicable method or simpler baseline, then re-evaluate the claim. |
| Sources share an origin or leave a material gap | Seek a different primary lineage or raw source; qualify the result if unavailable. |
| Criticism is unsupported or relies on a broken check | Reject or suspend it with the reason; repair the check only if doing so can affect the decision. |
| Evidence cannot presently separate the claims | Keep the disagreement unresolved and name the necessary access or observation. |

Recheck the affected conclusion after new evidence or a revision. Reopen independent exploration when a changed framing or material omission risk warrants it; use targeted confirmation when only an already-understood correction needs checking. Preserve earlier valid findings and restrictions instead of resetting the whole investigation.

Authorize a further pass only when its expected information gain and decision value justify its cost within actual limits, reassessing the planning allowance when needed. Record what changed since the prior pass, the reason for any revised allocation, what would stop or return this attempt, and the outcome and observed usage. Do not repeatedly send the same evidence and method to fresh workers hoping for agreement. Conversely, do not stop at the first synthesis when a material contradiction has an affordable, authorized discriminating check. When a limit prevents one action, preserve other useful permitted inquiry; if actual limits prevent further useful authorized work, return the partial judgment, unresolved evidence, and reopening condition, keeping unmet acceptance conditions explicit.
