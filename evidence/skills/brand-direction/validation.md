# Brand Direction review and reference reuse

The first cycle followed visual choices from the opening impression into use. Reviewers compared how products could share a material and still be recognizable, what information a color or line brought forward, and whether the chosen treatment survived selection, saving or a change in reading width. They preserved competing interpretations where the task changed the preference. See the [matched-material study](#shared-subject-different-identity), [selection and wrapping trials](#selection-against-neighboring-colors-and-the-measure-of-a-short-instruction) and [cross-project reference review](#curating-design-references-across-projects).

## Context and applicability

The [skill](../../../skills/brand-direction/SKILL.md) develops a product identity on real working surfaces. An existing-product pilot preceded eleven collections of eight synthetic studies. Two questions motivated the first collection review described here. Can a reviewer of one task find an art-direction problem that a whole collection makes easy to overlook? Can a later brief retrieve a relevant work without anchoring every new search to the same sources?

A prior collection had four pair reviewers and one collection reviewer. The extension used eight new synthetic briefs and retained four pair reviewers while increasing collection reviewers to four. The tasks, images and panel composition changed between collections. Differences between the two collections cannot establish an improvement in design quality.

## Evidence and evaluation

### The atmosphere a reviewer wanted to keep

An existing-product pilot began with a manually made light and dark pair. The product reviewer kept the contrast between modes and preferred the earlier dark reading scene to a later one with a prominent activity card. Two readers then saw the same scenes and request, with the reviewer's preference withheld. The reader using the earlier instructions favored the later scene for its paragraph flow. The reader using the revised instructions chose the earlier atmosphere and proposed reducing the borders around answers. Each version had one reader. Their disagreement showed how a plausible readability argument could lead to a different visual recommendation.

The product reviewer later accepted a refined night atmosphere as a starting point and liked the weathered texture, while finding the graphic too large. The specific mark, its size and motion, and adoption in the product remained open. The prototype could return from a terminal view to the passage being read. The handoff was checked separately against the product’s fixed-width terminal cells, reading states and navigation. This pilot supports recording precisely what the reviewer liked. Long-session readability and a redesign running in the product remain untested.

### Follow-up on recurring color across unrelated briefs

A reviewer noticed a dull yellow cast in the second collection. Inspection of its final frames supported a particular cause. The four desktop opening shares ranged from 56.8% to 95.9%. Highly colored subjects in two of those frames still sat within a much larger paper-colored field. Other studies in the same collection had dark surroundings. For this comparison, pale warm pixels had OKLCH lightness at least 0.75, chroma from 0.008 through 0.085 and hue from 65 through 115 degrees inclusive. The thresholds were fixed before measurement. The denominator was the viewport, including illustrations and empty space.

Eight new synthetic briefs drew on eight maker or research sources whose photographs, screenshots or publication figures the author inspected. Colors that look good do not have a target pixel distribution. Different briefs and participants limit comparison between collections.

## Outcome and reuse

This record owns the generalized evidence about those methods.

The studies support comparing the same task and content under competing visual treatments and retaining a dissenting judgment when a different task makes it relevant. A selected atmosphere can guide a visual refactor.
